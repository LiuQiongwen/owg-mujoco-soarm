# Ruled-Out Methods — Honest Negative-Results Ledger

This document exists to be quoted directly (condensed) into the T-RO paper's Future Work /
negative-results section. Every entry below was tested with a paired-trial design and
McNemar's exact test (or the noted equivalent), per this project's established statistical
convention. Nothing here was hidden or spun; this is the actual record.

## What this table is for in the paper

Framed correctly, this is not eleven failures — it's evidence of a disciplined, falsifiable
methodology: every mechanism was tested with a paired design and a real significance test,
negative results were reported as negative (not hidden or reframed), and the one true positive
finding in this whole investigation (the gripper-controller double-scaling bug, see the
methodology section) was found precisely *because* the team kept probing "why does this still
fail" instead of accepting a plausible-sounding partial explanation.

**Rows 7-10 were re-verified 2026-07-17** after discovering (independently of this table, while
testing an unrelated causal-validity question) that `PiperMultiObjectScene`'s placement sampler had
been unseeded this whole project, invalidating the pairing every McNemar comparison here depended
on. Re-running all four mechanisms under the fix produced a materially different, more precise
story than the original "four mechanisms, all negative" framing: **three of the four (v1, v2, v3)
turn out to have no measurable effect at all under valid pairing** — their original "made things
worse" findings were very likely themselves artifacts of the same RNG bug, which added spurious
between-condition variance that looked like a real effect. **Only v4 (pre-narrowing the gripper
before descent) has a genuinely negative, replicated effect** — and it is now far more strongly
significant (p=0.0005) than originally reported (p=0.11, not significant).

The corrected, honest framing: three contact-*response* interventions (react to detected contact
during descend) are inert — they don't meaningfully change outcomes either way, most likely
because they rarely trigger in a way that matters for this failure mode. The one intervention that
changes baseline geometry *before* any contact occurs (pre-narrowing) is the one with a real,
replicated cost. This is a narrower, more mechanistically precise negative result than the original
"everything we tried backfired" story, and it is the version that should be used in any paper
material — the original rows 7-9 numbers are superseded, not just supplemented.
