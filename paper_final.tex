\begin{abstract}
Open-world robot grasping requires both a diverse set of physically plausible
candidate poses and a criterion for selecting the best candidate at execution
time.
Existing pipelines decouple these concerns: a generative or heuristic sampler
proposes candidates, and a learned reranker selects among them.
We show that the quality of the \emph{generator} is the dominant bottleneck:
random center-of-mass (CoM) sampling supplies candidates that miss high-success
regions for geometrically complex objects, limiting what any downstream
reranker can achieve.
We replace the random sampler with an \textbf{Optimal-Transport Conditional
Flow Matching} (OT-CFM) model trained on 2,853 successful grasps collected
across seven YCB objects and 200 environment seeds.
OT-CFM maps Gaussian noise to the distribution of successful 6-DoF poses via
a single neural ODE, using optimal transport coupling to eliminate wasted
training signal from random source--target pairings.
At inference, five candidates are drawn from OT-CFM and reranked by a
lightweight geometric reranker (LGGSN).
On 175 paired trials (7 objects $\times$ 25 seeds, MuJoCo/SO-ARM101), this
pipeline achieves \textbf{94.3\%} success rate versus \textbf{82.3\%} for
random-CoM + LGGSN (${+12.0}$\,pp, $z{=}3.49$, $p{<}0.001$).
Ablations reveal that the OT coupling is the essential component
($-15.4$\,pp without it; standard CFM drops \emph{below} baseline),
geometric reranking contributes $-9.7$\,pp, and SAM visual conditioning
yields no significant gain ($+1.7$\,pp, $p{=}0.46$), enabling a
simplified deployment without SAM that achieves 96.0\%.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
6-DoF grasping, flow matching, optimal transport, grasp reranking,
generative models, robot manipulation, YCB benchmark
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Language-conditioned grasping requires an agent to map a natural-language
object description to a physical manipulation action that succeeds against
real object geometry.
A prevalent design pattern is \emph{propose-then-rank}: a heuristic or
learned candidate generator produces a set of grasp poses, and a reranker
selects the best one from that set~\cite{mahler2017dexnet,fang2020graspnet1b}.
The implicit assumption is that the generator supplies at least one
high-quality candidate; the reranker's task is only to identify it.

This assumption fails when the generator and the object geometry are
mismatched.
In top-down tabletop grasping on the SO-ARM101 manipulator, random sampling
centered on the object CoM with a fixed 6\,cm spread proposes positions that
systematically miss the narrow valid contact regions of flat (Scissors) or
elongated (CrackerBox) objects.
As a result, even a well-trained reranker cannot compensate: it can only
select the best of a bad set.

We reframe the problem as \emph{learning where successful grasps lie} rather
than selecting among random proposals.
Conditional Flow Matching (CFM)~\cite{lipman2022cfm} offers a particularly
attractive framework for this: it trains a neural vector field by regressing
on analytically computed interpolation paths from noise to data, avoiding
the multi-step denoising required by diffusion models.
Combined with Optimal Transport (OT) coupling~\cite{tong2023otcfm}, which
minimises the expected path length in each training minibatch, the model
receives consistently efficient training signal.

Our pipeline, illustrated in \cref{fig:pipeline}, combines an OT-CFM grasp
generator with an LGGSN geometric reranker.
The generator is trained on 2,853 labeled successful grasps collected via
per-candidate physical execution across seven YCB objects and 200 random
seeds.
At inference it draws five pose candidates from the learned distribution,
conditioned on a mean per-object visual feature; the reranker selects the
top candidate from geometric context.

\noindent\textbf{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{OT-CFM grasp generator:} a flow-matching model trained on
    live physical execution data that learns the distribution of successful
    6-DoF poses conditioned on object identity.
  \item \textbf{Generation + reranking pipeline:} paired 175-trial evaluation
    showing 94.3\% vs.\ 82.3\% ($+12$\,pp, $p{<}0.001$) over random
    sampling on MuJoCo/SO-ARM101.
  \item \textbf{Structured ablation:} OT coupling contributes $-15.4$\,pp
    and LGGSN reranking $-9.7$\,pp; visual conditioning is dispensable,
    enabling a simpler deployment (96.0\% without SAM).
  \item \textbf{Failure analysis:} CrackerBox failures are attributed to
    distribution coverage; Scissors achieves 100\% after physics-configuration
    correction---providing concrete design requirements for future work.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Grasp Quality Estimation and Generation}

Classical grasp quality metrics such as those in Dex-Net~\cite{mahler2017dexnet}
evaluate analytic contact stability, requiring known object geometry.
GraspNet-1Billion~\cite{fang2020graspnet1b} and AnyGrasp~\cite{fang2023anygrasp}
extend this to dense 3-D point clouds, but still assume access to full scene
geometry at query time.
6-DOF GraspNet~\cite{mousavian2019graspnet6dof} first introduced a
variational generator for diverse grasp proposals, training a VAE on
point-cloud crops.
Our method differs by operating purely on pose-space coordinates with
physics-derived success labels, requiring no geometric feature extraction at
generation time.

\subsection{Generative Models for Grasping}

GraspLDM~\cite{barad2023graspldm} applies latent diffusion in a VAE pose space,
achieving diverse 6-DoF proposals from point clouds.
GraspSAM~\cite{noh2024graspsam} combines Segment Anything with grasp detection
to handle open-vocabulary queries.
Neither work couples an explicit optimal-transport objective during training,
which we show is critical: standard CFM without OT coupling drops \emph{below}
baseline (78.9\% vs.\ 82.3\%, \cref{sec:ablation}).
Diffusion Policy~\cite{chi2023diffusion} uses conditional diffusion for action
generation but targets trajectory-level outputs with observation histories;
our setting is single-step pose generation conditioned only on object identity.
EquiGraspFlow~\cite{lim2025equigraspflow} adopts SE(3)-equivariant flow matching
directly on point-cloud inputs, providing strong geometric inductive bias for
arbitrary-orientation grasping.
Our approach differs in operating on a compact pose space ($\mathbb{R}^6$) rather
than raw point clouds, and replaces equivariance with explicit OT coupling---a
substantially lighter architecture that requires no equivariant backbone and
trains on modest execution-outcome data.

\subsection{Flow Matching}

Lipman~et~al.~\cite{lipman2022cfm} introduced Conditional Flow Matching
(CFM) as a simulation-free training objective for continuous normalizing flows.
Tong~et~al.~\cite{tong2023otcfm} generalised this to arbitrary source
distributions and showed that OT coupling---finding the joint distribution
$(x_0, x_1)$ that minimises expected displacement within each minibatch---
substantially improves convergence.
We are the first, to our knowledge, to apply OT-CFM in pose space to
6-DoF robotic grasp candidate generation; EquiGraspFlow~\cite{lim2025equigraspflow}
is a concurrent approach that instead uses SE(3)-equivariant continuous
normalizing flows conditioned on point clouds.

\subsection{Grasp Reranking}

Geometric reranking of grasp candidates from learned features has been explored
in DexGraspNet~2.0~\cite{fang2024dexgraspnet2} and CLIPort~\cite{shridhar2022cliport}
for task-conditioned selection.
LGGSN~\cite{owg2024} uses Bayesian Personalised Ranking (BPR)~\cite{rendle2009bpr}
to train a pairwise MLP reranker on episode-relative geometric features.
The reranker we use in this paper is the publicly available LGGSN v5d checkpoint
(17-dimensional features, object query embedding, validation pairwise accuracy 0.750).
Our contribution is to pair this fixed reranker with a generative
rather than random candidate supplier, showing that generator quality is the
dominant factor.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{System Overview}

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/pipeline.pdf}
  \caption{OT-CFM + LGGSN pipeline. At inference, the object CoM is
    estimated from the depth image, and OT-CFM generates five 6-DoF pose
    candidates conditioned on the object's mean visual embedding.
    LGGSN reranks by geometric context and the top candidate is executed.}
  \label{fig:pipeline}
\end{figure}

\cref{fig:pipeline} shows the full pipeline.
Given a language query (e.g., ``Banana''), the environment identifies the
target object, estimates its centre of mass, and retrieves a pre-computed
per-object SAM visual embedding.
OT-CFM draws $N{=}5$ pose samples from the learned success distribution.
Each sample is shifted to centre on the current CoM
(\cref{sec:inference}).
LGGSN reranks the five candidates using geometric and query-conditioned
features, and the top-ranked pose is executed.

\subsection{Training Data}
\label{sec:data}

We collect per-candidate execution data on MuJoCo/SO-ARM101 under
\texttt{physics\_weld\_after\_bilateral} mode.
For each (object, seed) episode:
(i) the object is spawned at a random tabletop position and settled;
(ii) $N_\text{cand}{=}30$ random CoM-centered pose candidates are sampled;
(iii) the top five are executed individually with object respawning
  between executions;
(iv) each candidate receives a binary success label.

This yields 7{,}000 execution records across 7 YCB objects and 200 seeds.
Of these, \textbf{2,853 (40.5\%) are successes} and are used to train the
OT-CFM model.
For each successful candidate, we compute a 256-dim SAM-base ROI feature by
projecting the grasp position $(x,y,z)$ onto the overhead camera image,
extracting a $3{\times}3$ window from the SAM ViT-B image encoder's
$64{\times}64$ feature map, and mean-pooling to a single vector.

\subsection{OT-CFM Grasp Generator}
\label{sec:cfm}

\noindent\textbf{Problem formulation.}
Let $p_\text{succ}$ denote the distribution over 6-DoF poses
$(x, y, z, \phi, \theta, \psi) \in \mathbb{R}^6$ for which the robot
successfully grasps the target object.
We aim to learn a conditional generative model $p_\theta(\cdot \mid c)$
that approximates $p_\text{succ}$ given object context $c$.

\noindent\textbf{Flow matching objective.}
Given source noise $x_0 \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I_6)$ and a successful pose
sample $x_1 \sim p_\text{succ}$, the CFM training path is:
\begin{equation}
  x_t = (1-t)\,x_0 + t\,x_1, \quad t \sim \mathcal{U}[0,1],
  \label{eq:path}
\end{equation}
with analytic conditional vector field $u_t = x_1 - x_0$.
The velocity network $v_\theta(t, x_t, c)$ is trained to minimise:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L} = \mathbb{E}_{t, x_0, x_1} \bigl[\|v_\theta(t, x_t, c) - u_t\|^2\bigr].
  \label{eq:loss}
\end{equation}

\noindent\textbf{Optimal transport coupling.}
Standard CFM pairs each $x_0$ independently with each $x_1$ in a minibatch,
leading to crossing flow lines that waste model capacity.
We replace this with \emph{minibatch OT coupling}~\cite{tong2023otcfm}:
within each minibatch of size $B$, we solve the Earth Mover's Distance
problem between $\{x_0^{(i)}\}$ and $\{x_1^{(i)}\}$ and reassign pairs
along the optimal transport plan.
This reduces expected path length, shortens flow trajectories, and---as
our ablation shows---is the single most impactful design choice
($-15.4$\,pp without OT, $p{=}0.001$).

\noindent\textbf{Conditioning.}
The context $c \in \mathbb{R}^{256}$ is the L2-normalised mean SAM visual
embedding for the queried object class, pre-computed from training data.
Our ablation (\cref{sec:ablation}) shows this conditioning yields no
statistically significant gain over zero conditioning ($+1.7$\,pp,
$p{=}0.68$), so the recommended deployment uses $c = \mathbf{0}$.

\noindent\textbf{Velocity network.}
The velocity network $v_\theta$ operates on the concatenated input
\begin{equation}
  [t_\text{emb}(3),\; x_t(6),\; c(256)] \in \mathbb{R}^{265},
\end{equation}
where $t_\text{emb} = [t,\,\sin(\pi t),\,\cos(\pi t)]$.
It is a 4-layer MLP with hidden dimension 512, SiLU activations,
and zero-initialised output layer, trained with Adam
($\eta{=}3{\times}10^{-4}$, weight decay $10^{-4}$, cosine schedule)
for 1000 epochs at batch size 128.
The best checkpoint is selected by lowest training loss.

\subsection{Inference: CoM-Relative Candidate Generation}
\label{sec:inference}

\noindent\textbf{Top-down constraint.}
The OT-CFM generator produces full 6-DoF poses
$(x, y, z, \phi, \theta, \psi)$; however, the current evaluation fixes
roll $= \pi$ and pitch $= 0$ (top-down constraint), reducing the effective
search space to 3-DoF $(x, y, \psi)$.
This constraint is applied post-generation by overriding the sampled roll
and pitch components before IK, and reflects the reach envelope of the
SO-ARM101 in the tabletop setting.
Lifting this constraint to full SE(3) is left as future work
(\cref{sec:future}).

At inference, we draw $N{=}5$ noise samples $x_0^{(i)} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I_6)$
and integrate the learned ODE for 20 Euler steps:
\begin{equation}
  x_{t+\Delta t} = x_t + v_\theta(t,\, x_t,\, c)\,\Delta t,
  \quad \Delta t = 0.05.
\end{equation}
The resulting poses $x_1^{(i)}$ are in the training coordinate frame.
To adapt to the current object position $(g_x, g_y)$, we apply a
\emph{CoM-relative shift}:
\begin{equation}
  x_\text{out} = x_1 - \mu_\text{train} + g_\text{com},
  \label{eq:shift}
\end{equation}
where $\mu_\text{train}$ is the global training-set mean pose and
$g_\text{com}$ is the current object CoM.
This preserves the learned yaw and spatial-offset distribution while
centering the candidates on the present object.
The vertical coordinate $z$ uses the live CoM height plus a fixed approach margin.

\subsection{LGGSN Reranker}

We use the LGGSN v5d reranker~\cite{owg2024} without modification.
LGGSN is a pairwise-trained MLP (BPR loss) that scores each candidate
using 17 geometric features including XY position, gripper opening width,
episode-relative height spread, distance from the point-cloud centroid,
point-cloud density features, and a 4-dimensional object query embedding.
The five OT-CFM candidates are scored, sorted descending, and the top-ranked
pose is executed.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Evaluation Protocol}

All experiments run on MuJoCo/SO-ARM101 with
physics-based bilateral contact verification as the grasp success criterion.
We evaluate on 7 YCB objects (Banana, TomatoSoupCan, Pear, MustardBottle,
Scissors, CrackerBox, PowerDrill) over 25 random seeds each,
giving \textbf{175 trials per condition}.
Success is defined as lifting the object above the table after gripper close.

\noindent\textbf{Baseline.}
Random CoM sampling ($x \sim g_\text{com} \pm 0.06$\,m,
$\psi \sim \mathcal{U}[-\pi/2, \pi/2]$, $w \sim \mathcal{U}[0.04, 0.09]$\,m,
same LGGSN v5d reranker) produces 5 candidates per episode.

\noindent\textbf{Statistics.}
We report Wilson score 95\% confidence intervals (CI) and two-sided
two-proportion $z$-tests to compare conditions.
Significance thresholds: $^{**}\!p{<}0.05$, $^*\!p{<}0.10$.

\cref{tab:main} reports the primary comparison.
OT-CFM + LGGSN achieves 94.3\% (165/175) versus the baseline's 82.3\%
(144/175), a statistically significant improvement of $+12.0$\,pp
($z{=}3.49$, $p{<}0.001$).
The 95\% Wilson confidence intervals are disjoint, confirming the gain
is not attributable to sampling variance.

The improvement reflects two complementary effects.
First, OT-CFM candidates concentrate near high-success regions for each
object, reducing the fraction of attempts that physically miss the target.
Second, LGGSN reranking then selects the geometrically best candidate from
an already higher-quality set.
Together, the generation--reranking pipeline achieves a 12\,pp absolute gain
over using either component alone with a poor generator
(see ablation, \cref{sec:ablation}).

\cref{tab:perobject} shows per-object results.
The largest gains accrue to objects where random CoM sampling covers the
valid contact region poorly.

\noindent\textbf{CrackerBox (+36\,pp, $p{=}0.019$).}
The box has a wide, flat top surface that requires the gripper to contact
along the long axis.
Random sampling at $\pm6$\,cm isotropically covers a $12{\times}12$\,cm
square, of which only the subset aligned with the long axis succeeds.
OT-CFM learns this asymmetric yaw prior (training yaw std 0.92\,rad,
generated 0.97\,rad), concentrating proposals in the effective region.
A separate contact-geometry diagnostic\footnote{Generation-isolated setup:
single-object scenes, semantic grounding disabled to remove VLM grounding
as a variable, 50 trials/object, OT-CFM candidates only. Three features
(local point density, surface-normal consistency, contact-width ratio) are
tested success- vs.\ fail-trial with Mann--Whitney $U$, corrected across
3 features $\times$ 5 objects (Pear, MustardBottle, CrackerBox,
TomatoSoupCan, PowerDrill; Banana and Scissors excluded as ceiling/invalid
respectively) via Bonferroni and Benjamini--Hochberg. This is a different
evaluation harness from \cref{tab:perobject} (no semantic grounding, a
different seed grid), so its numbers are not directly comparable to the
main table and are reported here only as corroborating evidence for
\emph{why} per-object gains differ.} corroborates this: local point
density under the gripper differs between successful and failed grasps
for CrackerBox (Mann--Whitney $p{=}0.0119$, significant under
Benjamini--Hochberg but not Bonferroni), consistent with success depending
on landing within a specific sub-region of the flat top rather than
anywhere on it.

\noindent\textbf{Pear (+28\,pp, $p{=}0.010$).}
The pear's rounded surface admits grasps in a broader but nonetheless
structured contact band; random sampling under-covers this region,
while OT-CFM generates proposals that fill it uniformly.
The same diagnostic finds this the strongest per-object signal measured:
surface-normal consistency under the gripper differs sharply between
success and failure (Mann--Whitney $p{=}0.00068$, survives Bonferroni,
large effect)---Pear's curvature makes grasp quality highly sensitive to
exactly where contact lands, matching why a learned, non-uniform proposal
distribution helps more here than on flatter objects.

\noindent\textbf{Banana (0\,pp), MustardBottle (0\,pp).}
Both objects reach the same ceiling under random and OT-CFM sampling,
leaving no measurable headroom for the generator to contribute.
Notably, the diagnostic finds MustardBottle has the \emph{strongest}
contact-feature signal of any object tested (local point density and
normal consistency both survive Bonferroni, $p{=}0.00030$ and
$p{=}0.00029$)---landing position clearly matters for MustardBottle's
grasp success, but random CoM sampling with LGGSN reranking already
reaches the achievable ceiling ($92\%$) in our main evaluation, leaving
OT-CFM no additional headroom to exploit that sensitivity.

\noindent\textbf{TomatoSoupCan (+8\,pp, $p{=}0.490$) and PowerDrill
(+12\,pp, $p{=}0.463$).}
Neither gain reaches significance at 25 seeds, but the diagnostic
distinguishes them: TomatoSoupCan's local point density survives
Bonferroni ($p{=}0.00123$, large effect), suggesting real but
seed-limited headroom, whereas PowerDrill is the only object with a
clean null across all three contact features under either correction
($p{\geq}0.17$)---consistent with its gain being smaller and directionally
weaker in \cref{tab:perobject}.
A post-hoc follow-up (single exploratory test, not part of the
pre-specified 15-test family above) checks a fourth, elongation-specific
feature for PowerDrill---gripper-axis alignment relative to the object's
principal axis---and finds only a marginal trend (Mann--Whitney
$p{=}0.062$, moderate effect): failed grasps skew toward the gripper
aligned perpendicular to this axis, successes toward parallel. We read
this as inconclusive rather than explanatory: PowerDrill's point cloud is
only weakly elongated (major/minor eigenvalue ratio $1.075$), so PCA's
``principal axis'' is not a strongly stable direction here, unlike for a
clearly elongated object. PowerDrill's null therefore remains the most
plausible reading of the original three features.

\noindent\textbf{Scissors (100\%, all conditions).}
Scissors achieves 25/25 across all methods after correcting a
physics-configuration bug: the original VHACD collision mesh
(14 merged convex hulls, $\approx$2\,cm thick) tunnelled through
the table under MuJoCo contact detection.
The corrected entry uses a bounding-box collision proxy scaled to
4\,cm thickness.
Since all methods reach ceiling (25/25), Scissors contributes equally
to each condition and does not differentiate methods.

\cref{tab:ablation} isolates each component's contribution.
The GRC-6DoF row adds a geometric-baseline comparison;
ablation rows decompose the full OT-CFM + LGGSN pipeline.

\noindent\textbf{GRC-6DoF is equivalent to random ($+0.6$\,pp, n.s.).}
Replacing random CoM sampling with GR-ConvNet-derived 6-DoF proposals
yields 82.9\%---statistically indistinguishable from the random baseline
(82.3\%).
Both methods supply similarly diverse candidate sets to LGGSN, leaving
no evidence that geometric candidate generation alone drives success.

\noindent\textbf{OT coupling is essential ($-15.4$\,pp, $p{<}0.001$).}
Replacing the OT sampler with random source--target pairing reduces SR
from 94.3\% to 78.9\%---\emph{below} the random-CoM baseline (82.3\%).
Random pairing forces the velocity network to reconcile crossing flow paths,
learning a blurred mean rather than a clean transport map.
OT coupling solves the global assignment within each minibatch, eliminating
crossings and enabling crisp, object-specific distributions.

\noindent\textbf{DDPM as a generative baseline ($-12.6$\,pp, $p{<}0.001$).}
To verify that OT coupling---not the flow-matching paradigm---drives
performance, we train an identical MLP with DDPM $\varepsilon$-prediction
(cosine schedule, $T{=}1000$, DDIM-50) on the same 2,853 successes.
DDPM achieves 81.7\% ($z{=}3.62$, $p{<}0.001$ vs.\ OT-CFM),
comparable to the random baseline and matching the remove-OT result.
The gap concentrates on CrackerBox ($36\%$ vs.\ $80\%$) and Pear ($68\%$
vs.\ $100\%$), where OT-CFM's straight transport paths preserve structured
multi-modal contact distributions that DDPM's isotropic diffusion erases.

\noindent\textbf{LGGSN reranking contributes $-9.7$\,pp ($p{=}0.003$).}
Without reranking (OT-CFM candidates executed in generation order),
SR drops from 94.3\% to 84.6\%.
The reranker adds meaningful value even when the generator already provides
high-quality candidates, by using geometric context (point-cloud density,
gripper--centroid distance) that the flow model does not have access to.

\noindent\textbf{SAM visual conditioning is dispensable ($+1.7$\,pp, $p{=}0.46$).}
Replacing the per-object mean SAM embedding with a zero vector yields
96.0\%---slightly higher than the full model, though not significantly so.
This is a practically important negative result: the OT-CFM model's
generative quality is driven entirely by learning from physical success
labels, not by the high-dimensional visual feature.
The recommended deployment therefore omits the SAM dependency
(saves $\sim$300\,MB GPU memory and one SAM forward pass per episode).

\noindent\textbf{Reliability across generation seeds.}
\cref{tab:ablation} compares methods at one generated candidate set per
orientation seed (25 orientation seeds).
To check whether the OT-CFM-vs.-alternatives ranking holds up under
independently resampled candidates, we ran a separate, generation-isolated
experiment (semantic grounding disabled, single-object scenes, 5
orientation seeds $\times$ 10 generation seeds per object, the same
OT-CFM/CFM-noOT/DDPM checkpoints).
Pairing trials by (object, orientation seed, generation seed) and testing
with McNemar's exact test, OT-CFM is significantly \emph{less} reliable
than both alternatives specifically on Pear ($p{=}0.041$ vs.\ CFM-noOT,
$p{=}0.021$ vs.\ DDPM) and TomatoSoupCan ($p{=}0.012$, $p{=}0.002$), with
no significant difference on the remaining objects.
We report this as a limitation rather than a contradiction of
\cref{tab:ablation}: the two experiments differ in evaluation harness
(semantic grounding on vs.\ off) and seed structure and are not directly
comparable, but the seed-resampled result indicates OT-CFM's per-object
advantage is not uniform across generator draws and should not be assumed
to generalize to every resampling of the generator.
An ensemble candidate-selection strategy substantially mitigates this for
both flagged objects: drawing 10 independent OT-CFM candidates (same
generation-isolated harness) and executing the one closest to their
pose-space consensus (median), rather than the one with the lowest
predicted IK error, raises Pear's success rate from 6\% to 68\% (Fisher's
exact $p{=}5.8{\times}10^{-11}$) and TomatoSoupCan's from 34\% to 64\%
($p{=}0.0048$), at matched ensemble size (10 vs.\ 10, so the effect is
attributable to the selection rule and not pool size). MustardBottle and
CrackerBox---which show no significant seed-reliability gap above---show a
correspondingly weaker (MustardBottle, $50\%{\to}68\%$, $p{=}0.10$) or
absent (CrackerBox, $44\%$ vs.\ $44\%$, exact tie) benefit from the same
strategy, consistent with the mitigation targeting specifically the
objects where single-draw reliability is worst.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Why OT Coupling Matters}

The failure of standard CFM below baseline ($78.9\% < 82.3\%$), and
the matching performance of DDPM ($81.7\%$), together confirm that the
gain in OT-CFM is not attributable to the flow-matching paradigm per se.
The critical ingredient is the optimal-transport coupling.
With only $\sim\!400$ training examples per object, random pairing produces
crossing flow paths; the velocity network learns a blurred mean rather
than a clean transport map.
OT coupling, by solving the global assignment within each minibatch,
eliminates these crossings and enables crisp, object-specific distributions.

\subsection{Why Visual Conditioning Does Not Help}

The SAM ROI features used for conditioning are averaged over all training
episodes for a given object class, yielding a fixed class-level embedding
rather than an instance- or scene-level one.
Such conditioning conveys no information beyond object class identity,
which the generation model can also infer from the training data distribution
itself.
A conditioning signal that varies \emph{within} an object class---e.g., a
point-cloud occupancy map or real-time depth image---might provide genuine
scene-specific guidance.
We leave this for future work.

\subsection{Scissors: Physics-Configuration Correction}

Scissors was 0/25 across all conditions due to a VHACD mesh tunnelling bug:
14 merged convex hulls at $\approx$2\,cm thickness allowed the object to
pass through the table under MuJoCo contact detection.
Replacing the geometry with a bounding-box proxy at 4\,cm restores
25/25 = 100\% for all methods, leaving per-condition $\Delta$pp values
unchanged.
A second, unrelated issue also affects Scissors in a separate, supplementary
generation-isolated diagnostic experiment (not reported in this paper): the
object-name matching used to condition OT-CFM sampling never matches
``scissors'' against its trained category key, silently falling back to
random sampling in that other experiment. This does not affect any number
reported here---Scissors is at ceiling (100\%) for every condition in this
paper regardless of which sampler produced the candidates---but we note it
for transparency since both issues share the same object.

\subsection{Limitations}

\noindent\textbf{Top-down constraint.}
The current pipeline fixes roll $= \pi$, pitch $= 0$, reducing the 6-DoF
problem to effective 3-DoF ($(x,y,\psi)$).
Extension to arbitrary gripper orientations requires either SE(3)-equivariant
flow matching or a richer orientation parameterisation (e.g., quaternion or
$6$-d representation).

\noindent\textbf{Simulation only.}
All results are from MuJoCo simulation.
Sim-to-real transfer requires alignment of the physical SO-ARM101's
calibration, contact dynamics, and depth-camera intrinsics with the
simulation geometry.

\noindent\textbf{CoM-relative shift.}
\cref{eq:shift} centres generated candidates on the current object CoM
by subtracting the training-set global mean.
This assumes the training object-placement distribution is roughly centred,
which holds for our setup but may break for out-of-distribution workspaces.

\subsection{Future Work}
\label{sec:future}

SE(3)-equivariant flow models (e.g., using Riemannian flow
matching~\cite{chen2024rfm}) could extend this approach to full 6-DoF.
As an immediate next step, we plan to validate the pipeline on a Piper 6-DoF
manipulator, which relaxes the top-down constraint and provides a direct test
of whether OT-CFM proposals remain viable under arbitrary gripper orientations.
Real-robot evaluation on the physical SO-ARM101 is an additional validation step.
Instance-level visual conditioning---using the point cloud of the segmented
target object as $c$---may provide meaningful additional signal for
cross-instance variation within object classes.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented a grasp generation pipeline that combines Optimal Transport
Conditional Flow Matching (OT-CFM) with geometric reranking (LGGSN) for
open-world 6-DoF grasping.
On 175 paired trials across seven YCB objects on MuJoCo/SO-ARM101,
the pipeline achieves 94.3\% success rate---a statistically significant
+12\,pp improvement over random CoM sampling with the same reranker
($p{<}0.001$).

Ablation analysis establishes that OT coupling is the essential component:
removing it drops performance below baseline, while standard CFM without OT
actively harms results.
LGGSN reranking adds 9.7\,pp on top of generation alone, confirming that
generation and reranking are complementary.
SAM visual conditioning provides no significant benefit, enabling a simpler
SAM-free deployment that achieves 96.0\%.

These results position OT-CFM as a practical candidate generator for
grasping pipelines: it trains on modest physical-execution data, runs as a
fast single-pass neural ODE at inference, and dramatically widens the
distribution of attempted grasps for geometrically demanding objects.
The geometric-baseline comparison (GRC-6DoF + LGGSN: 82.9\%) shows that
improved candidate geometry alone is insufficient; it is the
\emph{learned} OT-coupled distribution that drives the gain.
